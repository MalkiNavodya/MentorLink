# MentorLink
## Stakeholder Analysis

Project Name: MentorLink

Project Type: Personal Portfolio Project

Prepared By: Malki Navodya

Version: 1.0


1. Introduction

Before starting the development of MentorLink, it is important to understand who will use the system and how they will benefit from it. Every software application is built to solve problems for specific users, and those users are known as stakeholders.

MentorLink is designed to create a learning community where university students can connect with each other to exchange knowledge and develop new skills. Some users will join the platform to learn from others, while some will contribute by sharing their own knowledge. There will also be an administrator responsible for managing the platform.

This document identifies those stakeholders and explains their responsibilities, expectations, and the value they receive from the system.


2. Primary Stakeholders

Primary stakeholders are the people who interact with MentorLink on a regular basis. They directly influence the success of the platform because every feature is designed around their needs.


2.1 Student (Learner)

The learner is a university student who wants to improve existing knowledge or learn a completely new skill from another student.

For example, a first-year IT student may want to learn Laravel before starting an internship. Instead of searching through different social media groups, the student can use MentorLink to find experienced students who are willing to mentor others.

Main Activities

- Create an account
- Complete personal profile
- Search for mentors
- View mentor profiles
- Send mentoring requests
- Participate in mentoring sessions
- Rate and review mentors


Expectations

- Find suitable mentors quickly
- Learn from trusted university students
- Improve technical and soft skills
- Build academic and professional confidence


2.2 Student (Mentor)

The mentor is also a university student but has knowledge or practical experience that can be shared with others.

Rather than keeping their knowledge to themselves, mentors use MentorLink to guide fellow students, answer questions, and help them achieve their learning goals.

Mentoring also helps students improve communication skills and build leadership experience, which are valuable for future careers.

Main Activities

- Create mentor profile
- Add skills they can teach
- Accept or decline mentoring requests
- Conduct mentoring sessions
- Communicate with learners
- Receive ratings and reviews

Expectations

- Share knowledge with other students
- Build a strong reputation
- Improve teaching and leadership skills
- Expand professional networks


2.3 System Administrator

The administrator manages the overall platform and ensures that MentorLink remains safe, organized, and reliable.

Unlike students, administrators do not participate in mentoring sessions. Their role is to maintain the quality of the platform and solve issues that may arise during operation.

Main Activities

- Manage user accounts
- Manage skill categories
- Monitor reported content
- Remove inappropriate information
- Generate reports
- Maintain system security

 Expectations

- Keep the platform organized
- Ensure users follow community guidelines
- Protect user information
- Improve overall user experience


 3. Secondary Stakeholders

Secondary stakeholders are people or organizations that may not use MentorLink every day but still benefit from the platform.


 3.1 Universities

Universities encourage students to work together, participate in extracurricular activities, and improve their practical knowledge outside the classroom.

MentorLink supports these goals by creating an environment where students can learn from one another and build stronger academic communities.

Although universities are not direct users of the platform, they benefit from students becoming more skilled and collaborative.


3.2 Future Employers

Employers are always looking for graduates who possess both technical knowledge and communication skills.

Students who actively mentor others demonstrate leadership, teamwork, responsibility, and problem-solving abilities.

Projects like MentorLink also allow employers to evaluate a student's practical software development skills through their portfolio.


 3.3 Project Developer

The developer of MentorLink is responsible for planning, designing, developing, testing, deploying, and maintaining the entire system.

Since MentorLink is a personal portfolio project, its purpose is not only to solve a real-world problem but also to strengthen full-stack development skills and gain experience with modern software engineering practices.

 Responsibilities

- Plan project requirements
- Design user interfaces
- Develop backend APIs
- Develop frontend application
- Design database
- Perform testing
- Deploy the application
- Maintain project documentation

 Goals

- Improve Laravel development skills
- Improve Angular development skills
- Learn professional software architecture
- Build a strong software engineering portfolio
- Prepare for internships and future employment


 4. Stakeholder Summary

| Stakeholder | Role | Importance |
|-------------|------|------------|
| Student (Learner) | Learns new skills through mentoring | High |
| Student (Mentor) | Shares knowledge and guides learners | High |
| System Administrator | Maintains and manages the platform | High |
| Universities | Benefit from improved student collaboration | Medium |
| Future Employers | Evaluate students' skills and experience | Medium |
| Project Developer | Designs, develops and maintains the system | High |


5. Relationship Between Stakeholders

The learner and mentor are the core users of MentorLink.

Learners search for mentors based on specific skills and send mentoring requests. Mentors review those requests, communicate with learners, and conduct mentoring sessions.

The administrator supports both users by maintaining the platform, managing content, and ensuring a secure environment.

Universities benefit from improved student collaboration, while future employers may recognize students who actively participate in mentoring and knowledge sharing.


 6. Conclusion

Understanding stakeholders is an important part of planning any software project because it helps developers focus on real user needs instead of simply building features.

For MentorLink, student learners and mentors are the most important stakeholders because they represent the primary purpose of the platform—sharing knowledge and learning from one another.

By considering the expectations of each stakeholder throughout development, MentorLink can become a useful, user-friendly, and meaningful platform that encourages collaboration among university students while also serving as a professional portfolio project.
